Instrument gyros need to spin very rapidly. To get them to spin,and to keep the spin rate constant, gyros need a source of power.

Pneumatic gyro is poweered by a jet of air blowing onto the edge of the rotor. The edge of the rotor hassmall bucket-line identatons to catch the air.This helps to transfer the energy of the air jet to the rotor.

They are not supplied with air under pressure. Instead a vacuum pump (driven by the engine) removes air from the instrument casing. Pressure insade the case is BELOW ambient

The air jet is suplied via the gimbals. A filtered port connects to a duct that runs inside the gimbal via the bearings to deliver the air to the jet nozzle.

It is essential that the vacuum pressure is mantained, if its not,the gyro will slowly down and give unreliable readings.
It ismonitored using a vaccuumgauge. There is a warning if the pressure is too low.

They have a vacuum relief valve, when you have an oversuction,allowing the air to flow outside.
