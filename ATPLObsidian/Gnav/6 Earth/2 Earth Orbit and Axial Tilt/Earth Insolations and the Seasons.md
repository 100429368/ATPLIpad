Earth is warmedup by solar radiation from the sun, or isolation

The path of the Sun along its ecliptic across the surface of the Earth over the course of a solar year,commences when the sun is overhead the Equator atthe Sprint Equinox (March). It then moves east and north to the tropic of Cacer (N23.5º) at the Summer solstice (June), then east and sourth via the Equator again at the Autumnal Equinox (september) and to the Tropic of Capricorn (S23.5º) at the Winter Solstice (December), before moving north again to the Equator to repeat the process. 

The position of the Sun relative to the surface of the Earth changes over the course of the year.

Insolation varies over the surface of the Earth throughout the year mostly because of two factors.
1. **The tilt of the Earth**: The combination of the Earth's tilt and its changing position on the ecliptic causes the position of the sun relative to the surface of the Earth to change throughout the year Longer days mean more terrestrial heating.
2. **The changing distance from the Sun**: The Earth's elliptical orbit around the Sun means that its distance from the Sun changes throughout the year. When the Earth is closest to the sun, 147million km at Preihelion (early January),the Sun is overhead at the southern hemisphere . When the Earth is furthes fromthe Sun,152 million km at Aphelion (early July) the Sun is overhead the northern hemisphere.

Cumulatively,these result in wide variations in terrestrial heating from one hemisphere to the other.
